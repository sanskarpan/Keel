# K4.3 budget and quote evidence

## Scope delivered by this slice

This is a persistence and accounting boundary, not a provider integration. The package `internal/ai/budget` computes a deterministic worst-case quote from an immutable `policy.PolicySnapshot` and a code-owned rate card. The new PostgreSQL migration `0024_ai_budget_ledger.up.sql` stores period accounts, one admission and reservation per inference, immutable usage observations, and reconciliation attribution. No live model call or queue dispatch is mounted here.

## Quote rules

- Rate cards are selected by exact provider ID/version and model ID, within a half-open UTC effective interval. Empty, expired, overlapping, or unsupported cards fail closed.
- A quote carries its quote time and effective interval in the stored admission. Its stable digest excludes observation time, so requoting unchanged policy/rate/cost inputs preserves idempotency. The admission transaction checks the database clock: the card must still be effective, the quote may be at most 30 seconds old, and up to 5 seconds in the future is allowed for clock skew. Existing idempotent retries replay their original reservation even after the quote/period expires.
- Cards are copied into the rate book; timestamps are normalized to UTC with monotonic clock data removed before digesting. Equivalent instants therefore produce the same digest.
- The quote accepts only a policy snapshot. Its maximum input byte bound is conservatively treated as one token per byte because this slice has no tokenizer. The installed output token bound is used directly. Callers cannot override either ceiling or the prices.
- Inputs and outputs use integer micro-USD per million tokens. Every division rounds upward; a versioned basis-point safety margin is also rounded upward. Arbitrary-precision intermediate arithmetic rejects quote values that do not fit the persisted signed 64-bit per-attempt amount. PostgreSQL numeric counters retain cumulative committed/reserved totals beyond signed 64-bit range so a confirmed charge is never lost to counter overflow.
- Quote identity binds the policy digest, rate-card digest and exact provider/model identity. `Quote` fields are private and its string/debug representations contain no prompt or response data.
- The bundled `offline-test@v1` rate card and all test policy inputs are synthetic. They do not claim accuracy for any commercial provider. A production rate-card owner, review/approval workflow, refresh cadence, and alerting for approaching expiry remain unqualified.

## Admission and settlement invariants

- The budget account is provisioned separately. Admission requires the exact pre-provisioned tenant, currently active period ID, UTC interval, currency, and inference scope; the inference app role cannot create accounts or change hard limits.
- Admission locks the selected account row and the single tenant/scope active-period head. Only that pinned period can admit new work, so overlapping/misprovisioned account intervals cannot expose multiple independent caps. The database clock must also fall inside the period. Admission atomically checks `committed + reserved + new maximum liability <= hard limit`; it then inserts the immutable admission and reservation and increments the reserved counter. Composite foreign keys bind each reservation and usage observation to one exact inference/attempt/period. Same-identity retries replay the original reservation before active-period/quote expiry checks; conflicting request, principal, attempt, policy, rate-card, quote, or period identity is rejected.
- The active-period head is locked by `keel_meta.lock_ai_budget_period`, a tenant-checking `SECURITY DEFINER` function owned by the schema role. The app retains SELECT-only table access and cannot request a direct row lock (which would require UPDATE privilege); migration `0026` supplies the narrowly scoped function and schema-owner RLS policy.
- This slice admits one chargeable provider attempt per inference. It has no retry or fallback path. Any future multi-attempt path must aggregate-reserve every attempt's worst case or durably top up before dispatch.
- The reservation's period ID is immutable. Settlement joins through that admission and updates the original account even after a newer period is provisioned.
- A confirmed provider charge is appended at its full amount, even above the quote or remaining hard limit. The reserved amount is released, committed usage is incremented without clamping, and the account is marked blocked if either ceiling is exceeded. New admissions fail closed while blocked.
- Definite no-charge settles at zero. An ambiguous outcome appends an `unknown` ledger observation and retains its entire original reserve. A later result cannot use the normal settlement API; it must pass the injected, fail-closed reconciliation authorizer. Reconciliation appends a second immutable row with actor and reason, releases the original reserve, and records any actual charge in full. A provisional `estimated` entry may be appended while liability remains unknown; it does not change committed or reserved counters.
- Settlement retries with the same source reference and amount are idempotent. A conflicting retry is rejected. Usage and control evidence tables reject update/delete, including direct SQL mutation.
- An authorized correction to a settled charge is a signed append-only adjustment linked by inference/attempt and an opaque credit/debit source reference; it changes the committed counter without rewriting the original row. A separate `keel_budget_control` role and authorizer can raise a period cap after review, provided the new cap covers all committed and reserved liability; a stable event ID makes the restricted `SECURITY DEFINER` operation replayable, and the database function performs the cap change and immutable actor/reason event atomically. Direct table updates/event inserts are denied to the control role, and the normal application role cannot change hard limits or clear an overrun block. Production must confine this database capability to the reviewed control service; the verified authorizer remains an application boundary and no user-facing route is mounted.
- Source references are bounded opaque provider receipt/request identities. The caller must not put prompts, response bodies, customer data, or credentials in them. Provider request ID generation and a production reconciliation authority are not mounted in this slice.

## PostgreSQL and test evidence

`repository_integration_test.go` uses the least-privilege app role for admissions and settlements, a separately scoped budget-control role for authorized top-ups, and the disposable CI database administrator only to provision test accounts. It also verifies direct row-lock denial, transaction tenant matching, and the missing-head response of the period-lock function. It covers:

- eight simultaneous distinct admissions against a one-admission budget boundary;
- rejection of a future period and a quote older than the admission freshness bound;
- rejection of an unselected overlapping period and replay of an existing reservation after period rollover;
- recording a confirmed charge when the cumulative counter exceeds signed 64-bit range;
- replay of an identical admission and rejection of a reused attempt ID;
- no partial reservation/account-counter change after an admission uniqueness failure;
- retention of the reserve for an unknown outcome and denial of an unapproved normal settlement;
- authorized reconciliation, idempotent replay, and an actual charge above both quote and hard limit without clamping;
- blocking additional admissions after an overrun, then separately authorized/audited limit top-up and reopening;
- an idempotent append-only correction after settlement;
- rejection of an adjustment that would make committed liability negative;
- original-period settlement after a next-period account exists;
- forced-RLS tenant isolation and rejection of ledger update/delete.

Unit tests cover quote rounding, policy-derived bounds, rate-card expiry and ambiguity, overflow rejection, and UTC-normalized digests. Local `go test ./internal/ai/budget`, `go vet ./internal/ai/budget`, and `git diff --check` pass. The integration test skips without `KEEL_TEST_DATABASE_URL` and `KEEL_TEST_ADMIN_DATABASE_URL`; contract CI must also supply `KEEL_TEST_BUDGET_CONTROL_DATABASE_URL` and pass against its disposable PostgreSQL instance before this slice is merged.

## Operational limits and rollback

This migration is additive and contains no destructive backfill. Rollback is forward-only: disable admission through the enclosing K4 runtime/configuration, preserve immutable ledger evidence, and deploy a later migration or application version. Do not drop the tables while any reservation, unknown liability, reconciliation, or audit requirement remains. Existing app binaries do not dispatch through this package, so the migration alone cannot invoke a provider.

Overrun alerts and on-call ownership, production account provisioning/reconciliation authorization, actual provider price qualification, persistence retention, and live runtime wiring remain release gates. Synthetic unit and CI fixtures are not production provider evidence.
