# K5.5.1 Tenant cost breakdown

Issue [#323](https://github.com/sanskarpan/Keel/issues/323) adds a read-only cost read model over the durable AI budget ledger built in [K4.3](K4.3-BUDGET-EVIDENCE.md) and [K4.4](K4.4-QUEUE-EVIDENCE.md).

## Read model

`internal/ai/budget` gains a `CostReader` with two tenant-scoped reads.

`CostBreakdown` returns the period summary: the period bounds, currency, hard limit, the four cost classes, whether the account is blocked, and the watermark. An empty period ID selects the tenant's current period head; a period the tenant cannot read is reported as unavailable rather than as an empty period.

`CostEntries` returns a bounded per-inference breakdown, newest activity first, so an operator can see which inferences produced a class of cost. A limit outside `[1,100]` is rejected instead of silently clamped.

`CostWatermark` identifies the ledger position a breakdown was computed from: the budget account version, the number of ledger entries in the period, and the newest ledger entry time. A reader can compare watermarks to detect that a breakdown moved without re-deriving every row.

## Cost classes

Every amount is derived from the durable ledger and reservations. Nothing is estimated, extrapolated or rounded beyond what the ledger states.

- **Confirmed** is the exact settled cost: the sum of the confirmed, reconciled and adjusted ledger entries. It is the same liability the budget account enforces against its hard limit.
- **Estimated** is the cost of attempts that carry a current estimate and no settled outcome. An estimate that was later confirmed, reconciled or settled as no charge is not counted again.
- **Unknown** is the exposure of attempts whose provider outcome is unknown. The ledger records those attempts without an amount, so the exposure is the reservation the attempt still holds.
- **Outstanding** is the exposure of admitted attempts that have not settled at all, held at their reserved amount.

An attempt is confirmed, unknown or outstanding, and an attempt with a current estimate is both estimated and outstanding: the estimate is the expected cost and the reservation is the remaining exposure. The two classes never describe the same money twice.

## Isolation and consistency

Both reads run inside one read-only repeatable-read tenant transaction. The tenant context is set by the platform transaction wrapper, and the ledger, reservation and account tables enforce forced tenant RLS, so a reader cannot observe another tenant's cost even with a valid session. The summary, the rows and the watermark therefore describe a single consistent snapshot: the rows cannot contradict the summary.

## Privacy

The read model carries cost classes, liability state, the tenant-scoped opaque inference identifier, period bounds, currency, limit and watermark values. It never returns prompts, responses, model or provider identifiers, rate-card contents, principal bindings, request digests, source references, ledger reasons or any customer content. Amounts are integer micro-USD in the ledger's single USD currency.

## Operational use

Operators can explain a tenant's AI spend as confirmed, expected and uncertain instead of a single number, and can compare the account's committed and reserved liability with the ledger-derived classes. The watermark shows whether a breakdown is current. These reads are the evidence used by threshold and anomaly alerting, but this slice delivers no alerting of its own.

## Limits

This is an internal read model only. It is not a mounted HTTP route, an application runtime, an exporter, a metric series or a dashboard product. It does not create billing statements, invoices, entitlements or meters, does not convert currency, and does not persist a period rollup: every read recomputes from the append-only ledger. It does not alert, and it does not qualify a provider, price card or account provisioning. The read paths carry no per-tenant metrics and are not wired into the fail-closed role dispatcher.

## Validation

- Unit tests cover the reader construction, unvalidated tenants and periods, unbounded entry limits, and stored values that cannot be represented as a bounded micro-USD amount.
- Real-PostgreSQL integration covers a tenant with a confirmed charge, an unknown attempt carrying an estimate, and an admitted attempt that never settled, asserting each class, the watermark and the period facts.
- Real-PostgreSQL integration covers tenant isolation: a second tenant with its own account reads zero cost and no rows, and cannot read the first tenant's period.
- Real-PostgreSQL integration covers a missing period, which is reported as unavailable rather than as an empty period.
- Merge and contract-run evidence will be recorded in `docs/CHECKLIST.md` after the implementation PR is merged.
