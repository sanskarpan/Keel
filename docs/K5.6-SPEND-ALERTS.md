# K5.6.1 Threshold and history-qualified spend alerts

Issue [#326](https://github.com/sanskarpan/Keel/issues/326) turns the tenant cost read model from [K5.5.1](K5.5-COST-DASHBOARD.md) into operational spend signals.

## Alert contract

`internal/ai/budget` gains a deterministic evaluation that turns one tenant's cost state into bounded, content-free alerts. Each alert carries a fixed code, a fixed severity, the amount in micro-USD, the threshold it crossed and the period it belongs to.

| Code | Severity | Raised when |
|---|---|---|
| `budget.limit_blocked` | critical | the budget account has blocked further admission |
| `budget.limit_warning` | warning | committed plus reserved liability reached the warning share of the hard limit |
| `cost.unknown_exposure` | warning | unknown liability reached its share of the hard limit |
| `cost.confirmed_threshold` | warning | settled spend reached the absolute threshold |
| `cost.spend_anomaly` | warning | settled spend reached the multiple of the tenant's own historical baseline |

Liability is the exposure the account still holds: unsettled reservations plus the reservations of attempts whose outcome is unknown. The estimated amount is the expected cost of some of those unsettled attempts, so it is never added to the exposure a second time.

Alerts are returned in a fixed order, blocking alert first, and the result is bounded by the policy's alert limit. The same state always produces the same alerts, in the same order.

## History-qualified anomalies

The anomaly check compares the tenant's settled spend with the tenant's own settled history. The period under evaluation is never part of its own baseline. The history must contain at least the policy's minimum number of past periods that carry settled spend; a baseline is the median of those periods.

The result states why the check did or did not fire, so an operator never reads a silent pass as a clean result:

- `qualified` — the baseline existed and settled spend reached the configured multiple of it.
- `insufficient_history` — the tenant does not have enough settled history yet.
- `zero_baseline` — the history exists but carries no settled spend to compare against.
- `within_history` — settled spend stayed inside the tenant's own history.

## Policy

Thresholds are an immutable, code-owned `AlertPolicy` with basis-point ratios, an absolute settled-spend threshold, an anomaly multiple, a minimum history length and an alert bound. Every threshold and count is validated before use, and the policy digest binds an evaluation to the exact thresholds that produced it. Ratios and the anomaly multiple are integer arithmetic, so an evaluation never depends on floating point.

## Privacy

An alert carries a fixed code, a fixed severity, amounts, a threshold and a period identifier. It never carries prompts, responses, model or provider identifiers, rate-card contents, principal bindings, request digests, ledger source references, ledger reasons or customer content. A tenant cost dashboard or alert surface must still enforce its own authorization, identity and audit review before it shows these values to a person.

## Operational use

An operator can answer three questions from one evaluation: is the account blocked, is spend approaching the limit, and is this period's spend unusual for this tenant. The anomaly verdict says whether the tenant's history was sufficient to answer the third question at all, so a new tenant is not reported as anomalous.

## Limits

This is an evaluation library. It delivers no alert: nothing is persisted, no notification is sent, no webhook or email path exists, and there is no on-call escalation or acknowledgement. The policy is code-owned and immutable; there is no durable per-tenant threshold configuration, no policy UI and no policy change audit. The evaluation is not a metric, a Prometheus series, a dashboard product or a statistical model: it has no seasonality, no population comparison and no false-positive calibration beyond the tenant's own median history. It is not wired into the fail-closed role dispatcher, and no runtime evaluates it on a schedule. The tenant cost read model it consumes remains an internal read model with no mounted route.

## Validation

- Unit tests cover policy validation for every bounded threshold, digest stability and digest sensitivity to a changed threshold, the threshold alerts, history qualification in all four states, the anomaly baseline, alert ordering and the alert bound, and the rejection of incomplete cost state.
- Real-PostgreSQL integration covers a tenant with three settled historical periods and a current period whose spend is far above the baseline: the anomaly qualifies with the expected median baseline, the settled-spend threshold is raised and no limit warning is raised far below the limit.
- Real-PostgreSQL integration covers a tenant with no qualifying history: the anomaly is reported as not qualified and no anomaly alert is raised.
- Merge and contract-run evidence will be recorded in `docs/CHECKLIST.md` after the implementation PR is merged.
